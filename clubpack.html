Why this exists

The gap
between gym
and home.

1

Coaches are time-poor and communication is hard

Progress reports, injury notes, parent check-ins — all of it competes with actual coaching time. Most of it doesn't happen, or happens poorly.

2

Parents are in the dark

Families invest significantly in gymnastics but have almost no visibility into how their child is actually progressing, or what they should be doing at home.

3

Athlete issues go undetected

Burnout, anxiety, soreness, problems at home — by the time coaches notice, the athlete is already struggling. There's no early warning system.

4

Home training is a missed opportunity

Most clubs have no structured way to extend training into the home. Athletes either do too much, too little, or the wrong things entirely.

5

Club owners are running on passion, not margin

Most gymnastics clubs were started by people who love the sport — not by businesspeople. They're fighting churn, managing difficult parent relationships, and trying to stay viable, often without the systems or support to make any of it easier. They got into this for the athletes. The admin, the complaints, and the retention anxiety were never part of the plan.

"Parents are paying thousands a year and have almost no idea what's happening. Coaches are exhausted and genuinely scared to have difficult conversations with parents. Club owners feel like it's impossible to please anyone.

And the athlete sits right in the middle of all of this — often used as a messenger between adults who should be talking to each other. Something needs to change."

— Kym Volp, Gymnastics Online founder

02 In Development — Pilot 2026

Progress
Reporting

A completely new way for coaches to communicate athlete progress to families — professional, fast, and effortless. No typing. No drafting. No awkward conversations in the car park.

Coach records a voice note — at the end of a session, or whenever there's something to share. 60 seconds is all it takes.

Platform does the rest — the recording is transcribed, structured, and formatted into a professional progress report automatically.

Parents receive it via the app — a clear, professional update on their child's progress, goals, and next focus areas.

Incidents and injuries too — the same process handles ad hoc reporting, incident notes, and injury documentation. Everything in one place.

Share moments, not contact details — coaches can send ad hoc messages, photos, and videos directly to parents through the platform. Milestone moments, funny gym clips, things worth celebrating — all shared professionally, without a coach ever needing to give out their personal phone number or social media details.

Club owners get full visibility — a dashboard showing what's been reported, flagged, and followed up. Nothing falls through the cracks.

03 In Development — Pilot 2026

Athlete
Wellness

A simple weekly check-in that gives coaches early warning signals — before small problems become serious ones. Gymnastics is a high-demand sport. The earlier you know, the better you can respond.

Athletes check in before every session — mood, energy, sleep, soreness, motivation. For younger athletes, parents check in on their behalf. Takes under two minutes.

Coach dashboard shows trends — individual athlete patterns and whole-squad snapshots. Flags surface automatically when something shifts.

When an issue is identified, coaches don't face it alone — alongside the alert, they receive a report and practical best-practice recommendations developed by expert coaches, physiotherapists, and psychologists. Guidance that's actually useful, right when it's needed.

Club owner visibility — aggregate wellbeing data across the whole club. Identify emerging patterns before they become serious safeguarding or retention problems.

Pilot clubs help shape how this feature works — your feedback directly influences the check-in questions, the dashboard design, and the alert thresholds.
