Understanding Gymnastics Levels in Australia: A Parent's Guide

Kym Volp
6 January 2026
12 min read

"I just want to understand what level she's in… and what it actually means." If you've ever sat in the parent viewing area feeling confused about where your child sits in the gymnastics system — you're not alone. Parents regularly wonder whether their child is progressing at the right pace, what the levels actually represent, and why it seems so hard to get a straight answer.

The reason it feels confusing is because it genuinely is complicated. In Australia, there is not one single 'levels system' used by every club. There are multiple pathways and competition frameworks operating side-by-side — and understanding which one your child is in makes all the difference.

The official national pathway: Gymnastics Australia's ALP

The most widely recognised structure is Gymnastics Australia's Australian Levels Program (ALP). This operates as Levels 1–10 and is divided into three distinct phases:

  • Foundation Programme (Levels 1–2): Skills-based, no formal competition focus. Emphasises basic shapes, strength development, flexibility, and movement safety.
  • Compulsory Levels (Levels 3–6): Set routines with required elements. Progress is intentionally gradual, prioritising technique mastery before advancing.
  • Optional Levels (Levels 7–10): Coach-customised routines within regulatory rules. Scores combine D-Score (difficulty), E-Score (execution), and neutral deductions.

What Foundation, Compulsory, and Optional really mean

Many parents hear their child is in "compulsory" and assume this just means the training is mandatory. It doesn't — it refers to the routines being compulsory, meaning every gymnast at that level performs the same set elements. This is intentional: it creates a consistent standard for judges and ensures gymnasts develop a common technical foundation before individualising their work.

When gymnasts move into Optional levels, routines become individualised. This is where coaching philosophy starts to show more clearly, and where the training environment, facility, and coaching staff have a bigger influence on what a gymnast is able to achieve.

Coach Insight

"A parent might say, 'My friend's daughter has her Level 5 badge but she's still competing Level 4.' The badge shows recognition of skills, but the competitive level is determined by readiness and state regulations. They're not the same thing."

Skipping levels vs progressing normally

The question of whether a gymnast can skip a level comes up often — and the answer is: sometimes, depending on state regulations and club policy. But even when it's technically permitted, coaches weigh up whether skipping creates technical gaps or safety concerns that will cause problems later.

Coach Insight

"Coaches aren't just thinking about whether a gymnast can do Level 6 skills. They're thinking about whether she has the physical preparation to handle the training load safely, whether her technique is clean enough to build on, and whether the jump would create gaps that cause problems later. Long-term development is always the priority."

The elite and international pathway: where it fits

Parents sometimes assume the ALP naturally leads into elite gymnastics — that Level 10 is followed by "elite" and then "international." It's more nuanced than that. The International Stream sits alongside the ALP but is not simply the next step after Level 10.

It includes Future International, Developing International, and Senior International stages, and is governed separately through state and national selection processes. Not every gymnast who completes Level 10 enters this pathway — and not every elite gymnast followed the standard ALP route to get there.

Other systems you may see in Australia

  • Gymstar: A flexible competitive programme with state divisions
  • Club Excel-style programmes: Inspired by overseas models with routine flexibility
  • State Stream programmes: Lower-hour competitive pathways alongside ALP
  • Independent competition leagues: Separate from GA national events
  • Recreational levels: Internal club badge systems for motivation

How to identify which system your child is in

Three questions that clarify it quickly:

  1. Is this the Gymnastics Australia ALP (WAG Levels 1–10)?
  2. Do competitions follow GA Technical Regulations?
  3. Are routines compulsory (Levels 3–6) or optional (Levels 7–10)?

Common questions parents ask

Does starting in ALP mean my child is on the elite pathway?
Not automatically. The International Stream operates separately with different governance and selection criteria.
Why are some kids the same age but in different levels?
Because gymnastics levels reflect readiness and development, not age alone. The ALP is designed to allow gymnasts to develop at a rate that suits their ability.
Why does my friend's program sound different at the same level?
They may be using a different system — Gymstar, Excel-style, State Stream, etc. — which operates under different rules and structures.
Can my child skip a level?
Eligibility depends on state regulations and club policy. Even when permitted, coaches evaluate whether it's safe and technically sound for long-term development.
How do coaching philosophies differ?
Some prioritise acceleration toward higher levels, while others focus on thorough skill mastery at each stage. Neither approach is inherently wrong — what matters is that it suits your child's development and safety.

A steadier way to think about gymnastics levels

It's completely normal to want your child to progress. But in gymnastics, the healthiest version of that hope sounds like this: "I want her to be safe, confident, and developing well — and I want to understand the system so I can support her without adding pressure."

Understanding gymnastics levels serves one purpose: supporting your child's development safely and confidently, and preserving their love for the sport for as long as possible.
